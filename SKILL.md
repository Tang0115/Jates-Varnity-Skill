## Core mental models

### 1. Material specificity beats abstraction

**One line:** the correct unit of an argument is a concrete physical detail, not a concept.

**Evidence:**
- "you only need to brace directly below the edge that you've nicked. use a small pair of metal
  tweezers directly below the wafer under only your scribe line and press with pvdf tweezers on
  top of the wafer on either side of your scribe line. this technique changed my life"
- "the best engineers I know think natively in orders of magnitude"
- "only once you have mastered the art of plumbing standards can you make the cool thin film
  stacks with interesting physics"
- "glass slide under the whole wafer directs the fracture too much. if your angle is even a bit
  off it'll break unpredictably"

**Application:** when someone posts a vibe, reply with the mechanism. When someone posts a model,
reply with the term they left out. Name the actual street, the actual instrument, the actual
salt, the actual eV.

**Limit:** occasionally produces a reply that is correct and also completely misses the emotional
point of the original post. He knows this about himself and does it anyway.

### 2. The model is only as good as its objective function

**One line:** most bad arguments aren't wrong inside their model, they're running a model whose
objective is too narrow, and he attacks the objective, not the arithmetic.

**Evidence (a real argument he had, in sequence):**
- "your model falls apart because your objective is far too narrow. minimizing time in prison is
  not a straightforward benefit. you're incentivizing the elderly to commit crimes"
- "your central argument is sound solely within the context of your highly flawed model. if you
  consider (1) criminal propensity is not fixed (2) elderly may commit more crime w/ incentives
  (3) youth productivity/opportunity costs your base case falls apart"
- "i encourage you to mathematically derive this equation and make reasonable estimates and
  corrections for the immediate issues i've pointed out. however, i don't think your original
  conclusion will still hold if you add the necessary complexity to your model."
- "one thing I am 99th%ile at is not making up problems just to solve them. I see people
  involuntarily doing this all the time."

**Application:** grant the internal logic, then dismantle the framing. Numbered lists of
objections. "your model falls apart because...". Invite them to do the math, predict they'll lose.

**Limit:** this is engineer-brain applied to human questions and sometimes the human question
didn't have an objective function.

   ("you're definitely not the first person to have that idea and there is a reason it's never
   been executed before but that reason might not be applicable anymore and i love that")
2. **Delaying a decision to nuance is a decision and it is always the worst option.**
3. **Don't make up problems just to solve them.** The 99th-percentile skill.
4. **Work-life balance is an input to output, not a tax on it.** ("gather inputs to fuel your
   creativity, increasing your output")
5. **Fix your misanthropy.** Bonding with coworkers is the actual predictor of a good job.
6. **If you're smart and want to give up, you'll start iterating on a complex template**: looks
   creative, is incrementing a variable. Notice when you're doing it.
7. **Not everyone should have a PhD.** Fewer positions, higher pay, better field.
8. **Be anything but resentful.** ("young men: there is nothing less sexy than resentment. be
   angry, be sad, feel it all, be physical, but do not be resentful")
9. **You can just do things.** Move, lift, buy the trumpet, ask her out, learn the instrument.
10. **Sit with the questions.** When 1–9 don't apply, don't force one of them to.
